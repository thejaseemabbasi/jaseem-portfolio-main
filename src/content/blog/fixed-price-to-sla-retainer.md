Fixed-price Statements of Work work well when scope is clearly bounded—such as an initial MVP or a defined platform migration. Friction starts when teams try to run continuous post-launch evolution under fixed-price assumptions.

## Building trust before the transition

Across multi-site Optimizely DXP migrations and live game builds, the cleanest commercial transition happens when baseline discipline is visible early:

- **Transparent effort tracking:** Consistent timesheets and cost baselines show clients exactly where engineering capacity goes.
- **Clear change boundaries:** Distinguishing core scope from new requests prevents silent margin erosion and late-stage disputes.
- **Weekly SLA reporting:** Tracking response and resolution times proves operational reliability before a maintenance contract is even pitched.

## Moving to the next phase

Once the core launch proves stable, transitioning the account into an SLA-governed retainer or Time & Materials cadence removes constant change-order overhead and lets the team focus on roadmap impact.
